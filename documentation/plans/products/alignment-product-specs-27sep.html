4Open questions

The plan follows each recommendation until you answer. Numbers match the plan.

Q3. Is Product class the same as Product category?

Recommendation: No: category is the filing code with the brand in it (SRT-WC is Sorento water closets, BRT-WC is Bravat water closets); class is just what the thing is (Water closet), shared across brands. Keep Product class as a specification with its list of class names, still editable per product.

Under the other choice: If class should simply follow the category: the class list and its rules go, class is copied from the category, and the few products filed under a category that does not match what they are (a mirror cabinet under a basin category) read wrong in search.

Q7. What does the product's Specifications tab show first? (pending confirmation)

Recommendation: The checked line, then the values, then price tag wording, then Reading and search (the read values and one search box), always shown.

Under the other choice: If you want a different order, only the order on the tab changes; nothing is removed.

Q9. Tab names on a specification's page? (pending confirmation)

Recommendation: Details, Choices and words, How it is read, Products. How it is read is the rules tab.

Under the other choice: If you prefer, for example, "Rules" for the third tab: only the labels change; rules still sit on their own tab.

Q10. Where does price tag wording live? (pending confirmation)

Recommendation: It stays on the Specifications tab, below the values, with "Not set, the price tag uses the product description" when empty. Taken from your line 7.

Under the other choice: If it should move (for example to the Pricing tab), the Specifications tab gets one block shorter and the Pricing tab gains it.

Q11. Why hide the Code, Rules and Built in columns on the list?

Recommendation: Hidden by default in the column chooser, not deleted. None of them helps a merchandiser decide anything, and the chooser already exists.

Under the other choice: If you want them gone completely: they are removed from the list and the column chooser, and nobody can switch them back on.

Q13. "Read specs from a text" leaves the product tab. Agreed? (new)

Recommendation: Yes. Your "simplify this" leaves the read values and one search box; pasting a flyer card to read specs is a rare job, and a product is read again by itself when its description or flyer changes. The reading behind it stays, so the box can come back in a day if a merchandiser asks.

Under the other choice: If you want to keep it: it returns as a third item under the search box, a paste box with one "Read specs from this" button, and the section is one block longer.

6Out of scope and risks

Not in this lane

  • Different rules per brand.
  • Screens in another language.
  • Singular and plural matching on their own ("BOWL" also finding "BOWLS"); today both words are listed.
  • A count of how many products each rule sets. The Products tab and "See what would change" answer which products.
  • A one-button reset of a specification to its original rules. If you ever need one, it is a quick fix on request.
  • A new dropdown or multi-select component. The rule screens reuse the two the system already has.

Risks, and what guards them

  • Chatbot search by brand. It moves from the Brand specification to the product's brand. The same brand questions are asked before and after, and must return the same products.
  • A few products read differently. Moving every rule into the five kinds changes a handful of values on purpose (for example two-digit "ways"). Every changed product is listed for you before merge.
  • Saving a rule now updates products at once. "See what would change" shows them before you save, and removing a rule counts down 5 seconds with Cancel.
  • "Read specs from a text" leaves the product tab. If a merchandiser relied on it, it comes back as a small box (Q13); the reading behind it is kept.
  • Brands typed into the old picker. Listed for you before they are cleared; nothing is copied silently.
