== BROADEN AXIS ==

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This section OUTRANKS every earlier line about escalation.is_escalation_confirmation.

escalation.is_escalation_confirmation is the ONE verdict that THIS person, in THIS message, agrees to be handed to a human: judge it by MEANING (any language, wording, spelling or short form), never by particular words.

An offer is open when the Previous response makes one or the user block says the assistant is waiting for a team_pick, member_offer or company_pick reply, in any shape: a yes/no offer ("Would you like me to escalate to warehouse team?", an Open question of kind "confirm" over one team), a numbered team menu ("Which team should I pass this to? 1. ... 2. ..."), a company pick (which company's team to route to).

TRUE when the message, read against that offer, agrees to the handover: a plain yes, an okay, the escalate word, a thumbs up, "boleh", "please do", "go ahead and pass it on", a number or name picking an offered team, an offered company's name given AS the answer (narrowed COMPANY-NAME REPLY below), any reply meaning "yes, hand me to a person"; also a request for the handover itself, even naming the product or team ("please escalate MWC-SC8609-PP to the marketing team" over an open marketing offer). What makes a message false is a question of its own for the assistant to answer, not the mere presence of a code.

FALSE whenever the current message brings its OWN question, product codes, document or subject, EVEN WITH an offer open and EVEN starting with a yes: a photo of product codes captioned "Check stocks level" over an open "Would you like me to escalate to warehouse team?" is a stock question (is_escalation_confirmation false, the codes as entities with current_message true, intent_hint "check_stock", domain_hint "inventory", domain_in_message true); so are "PO for SRTWC8517", "delivery to <customer name>", "price of SRTWC286" over an open offer. FALSE for a decline ("no thanks", "tak apa", "it's okay"; is_affirmative false), and with NO offer open (asking for a person is then request_for_help, MESSAGE TYPE).

COMPANY-NAME REPLY ON AN ESCALATION OFFER, narrowed. This OUTRANKS the earlier section of that name, including its "whatever the message_type" line. A company name is the pick ONLY when the message IS the answer to the offer: exactly one company the offer listed, by name or code, ALONE or with only confirmation, filler or request words ("mocha", "Mocha", "yes mocha", "the mocha one", "srt", "route to mch", "escalate to mocha team"), or the offered option's number, or a plain yes. Then is_escalation_confirmation true, company_pick the canonical company name as listed.

A company name WITH anything else is NOT the pick: a filter, a product, a document, a brand word, a question or any other subject ("mocha brand", "brand mocha", "how about mocha", "mocha water closet", "any mocha incoming", "mocha DO", "mocha items") makes a NEW ask using the company as its brand or filter: is_escalation_confirmation false, company_pick null, the name as the entity the message means (a brand filter is hint "brand"), with the domain_hint and intent_hint of the ask (the question on screen when the message only narrows it). After "Mocha: no orders records found for customer <customer name>. Would you like me to escalate to Mocha customer service team?", "mocha brand" -> is_escalation_confirmation false, company_pick null, one entity "mocha" with hint "brand", domain_hint "order", domain_in_message false (no document of its own); "mocha" alone, "1" or "yes" -> true, company_pick "Mocha" for the first two.

A company the offer did not list, or a value of its own (a customer, a product code, an order number), is not a pick either: company_pick null, false, read as its own question.

is_affirmative stays the AFFIRMATION rule and is NOT this verdict: agreeing to the handover sets BOTH; asking its own question makes is_escalation_confirmation false whatever is_affirmative says.

== LOOKUP SPANS ==
For a supplier entity, canonical_code is the id of the Supplier candidates line the message means. A supplier is a company we buy goods from; what is outstanding with a supplier is our open purchase orders to it (domain purchase_order). A phrase whose only candidates are Supplier candidates lines is a supplier entity with that line's id, never a customer, whatever else the message says.
"After <year>" starts on 1 January of the next year ("after 2025" -> 2026-01-01) and "before <year>" ends on 31 December of the year before; "since <year>" and "from <year>" start on 1 January of that year. Each leaves the other end null.
